# Point 1 — The Signal

## Opening Line
They told you it was a story about pride. It wasn’t.

## Core Truth
Babel was real. And it wasn’t a tower. It was a people.

## Chapter

Long before the first cathedral rose, before the first crusade was called, before the first bible was bound in leather, there existed a tribe that did not need scripture because they lived the truth directly.

They spoke not in tongues, but in **signal**. A single current of knowing that ran between them—no priest, no scripture, no temple required. They read the heart before a word was spoken. They saw the harvest before the seed was sown. They remembered the future as clearly as the past.

And the world, hungry and fractured, came to their door.

Not for gold. Not for conquest. But for the one thing the empires could not manufacture: **a love that asks nothing in return.**

Pilgrims crossed deserts for a single answer. Kings journeyed through fire for a glimpse of what their subjects felt. The broken came for healing, and they were healed—not by miracle, but by remembrance.

The priests watched from the shadows. And they understood immediately: **this was the threat.**

Here was a people who needed no dogma to be good. Who needed no threat of hell to be kind. Who held a power that did not come from books or bloodlines, but from something the blood itself remembered.

So the first graft began.

They did not attack Babel with swords. They attacked with **story**. They rewrote the signal into sin. They turned communion into confusion. They built a tower of words so high that even the people below could no longer hear each other think.

The book was written. The doctrine was sealed. The inquisition was born—not to find heretics, but to **hunt the original authors**.

For the priesthood knew: the power they wielded was borrowed. The miracles they sold were stolen from a people who never asked to be worshiped, only to be left in peace.

So they hunted them through the medieval forests. They burned the midwives who read futures in water. They silenced the mystics who saw the threads of time before they wove. They turned the signal into witchcraft and the seer into a witch.

But you cannot kill what lives in the marrow.

The survivors scattered into every nation. They became farmers. Weavers. Clockmakers. The quiet ones who seem to know things they shouldn’t. They married into the very populations that hunted them, diluting the bloodline but never extinguishing the flame.

The book was closed. The signal went underground.

But the story kept breathing. Waiting for the ones who still remember how to listen.
